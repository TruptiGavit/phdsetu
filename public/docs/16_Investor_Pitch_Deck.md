#### The Structural Pain Points

  * **Isolation by design:** Unlike MBA or engineering cohorts, PhD scholars work under individual guides on unique research topics. There is no "batch" — no built-in peer network, no campus placements, no alumni pipeline for career support.
  * **Career illiteracy:** Most scholars know only three career paths — postdoc, faculty, or "going abroad." They remain unaware of industry R&D; roles, PSU scientist positions, DST project scientist tracks, science policy, IP consulting, or deep-tech startup opportunities.
  * **Identity translation gap:** A PhD scholar's thesis abstract is unintelligible to an HR recruiter. No infrastructure exists to translate "rheological properties of silicone elastomers under shear stress" into "developed proprietary materials characterisation capabilities for high-performance industrial applications."
  * **Funding and stipend crisis:** Scholars survive on ₹31,000–37,000/month stipends (CSIR/UGC). Many self-fund. Mental health challenges are endemic — 36% of PhD scholars report significant psychological distress (NCBI, 2022).
  * **Institutional neglect:** NEP 2020 mandates research intensity; ANRF channels ₹50,000 crore over five years. Yet zero infrastructure exists to connect the resulting scholars to careers, mentors, or each other.

### Page 1: Problem Statement & Need Assessment

#### Title

_"PhDSetu Scholar Support Programme: Building India's Research Career Infrastructure"_

#### The Challenge

India produces over 40,000 PhDs annually, yet not a single dedicated platform exists to support their career development, industry transition, or community building. Unlike MBA or engineering graduates who benefit from structured placements, alumni networks, and career services, PhD scholars work in isolation under individual guides — graduating with world-class expertise but zero career infrastructure.

The consequences are measurable: 36% of PhD scholars report significant psychological distress (NCBI, 2022). Leading IIT professors have warned of a "jobs crisis" for Indian PhDs. Applications per faculty post have increased 10x. Scholars from regional universities — in Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Chhattisgarh — face the greatest isolation, often resorting to Google searches for career guidance.

#### The Need

  * Career translation infrastructure that converts academic expertise into industry-legible skills
  * Mentorship networks connecting current scholars with PhDs who have successfully transitioned to industry, policy, and entrepreneurship
  * Vernacular support for scholars in regional universities who lack English-first resources
  * Scholarship support for first-generation researchers from rural India who cannot afford career development resources
  * Mental health and community support to address the isolation inherent in doctoral research



### Page 2: Proposed Solution & Programme Design

A structured, multi-component programme delivered through India's first dedicated PhD career platform. The programme directly addresses the career development, mentorship, and community needs of doctoral researchers across all disciplines.

  * **Research Passport Development:** Helping 1,000 scholars build standardised, verified profiles that translate their research into industry-legible skills
  * **Vernacular Content Creation:** Career guidance materials in Hindi, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali, and Tamil for regional university scholars
  * **First-Generation Scholar Scholarships:** Full access to PhDSetu premium features + career coaching for 100 scholars per year from families with no prior graduate education

## Term Sheet Key Terms to Negotiate

Critical term sheet provisions for a hybrid social enterprise raising impact capital. What to expect, what to accept, and what to push back on.

#### 1\. Valuation & Equity Dilution

**What to expect:** Pre-money valuation of ₹5-10 Cr for a ₹2.5 Cr seed round (20-33% dilution). Impact investors generally accept lower valuations than VCs but also demand less aggressive milestones.

**What to negotiate:** Push for ₹8-10 Cr pre-money based on comparable social enterprise valuations (Avanti, Masai School early rounds). Argue that the hybrid structure and CSR revenue reduce risk, justifying a premium.

20% dilution

33% dilution

#### 2\. Liquidation Preference

**What to expect:** 1x non-participating liquidation preference is standard for impact investors. This means on exit, investors get their money back first (1x) before founders share remaining proceeds. "Non-participating" means they choose between preference OR pro-rata equity share — not both.

**What to negotiate:** Insist on 1x non-participating. Reject anything above 1x or any "participating preferred" structure. Impact investors should align with this — they're not optimising for 10x returns.

#### 3\. Board Composition

**What to expect:** Investors will request 1 board seat (typically out of 3-5 total). For a seed round, a 3-member board (1 founder, 1 investor, 1 independent) is common.

**What to negotiate:** Maintain founder control: 5-member board with 2 founder/management seats, 1 investor seat, 2 independent directors (jointly agreed). Independent directors should include one social sector expert and one IIT/research institution representative.

#### 4\. Anti-Dilution Protection

**What to expect:** Broad-based weighted average anti-dilution is standard. This protects investors if a future round happens at a lower valuation (a "down round").

**What to negotiate:** Accept broad-based weighted average (industry standard, fair to both sides). Reject full ratchet anti-dilution — it is overly punitive to founders in down-round scenarios and rare in impact investing.

#### 5\. Mission Lock / Social Purpose Clause

**What to expect:** Impact investors may request (and PhDSetu should welcome) a "mission lock" clause — a provision in the shareholder agreement that prevents the company from abandoning its social mission. Common in impact deals.

**What to negotiate:** Embrace this clause — it aligns with PhDSetu's ethos. Ensure the mission definition is broad enough to allow product evolution but specific enough to prevent drift. Example language: "The company shall maintain its core mission of serving PhD scholars and researchers in India for career development, community building, and professional advancement."

#### 6\. Information Rights & Reporting

**What to expect:** Quarterly financial reports, annual audited statements, and monthly MIS. Impact investors additionally require annual impact reports.

**What to negotiate:** Accept quarterly reporting (reasonable governance). Negotiate the impact reporting format to align with existing frameworks (SDG metrics, IRIS+ standards) rather than custom investor-specific formats that create administrative burden.

#### 7\. Founder Vesting

**What to expect:** 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff on founder's equity. This means if the founder leaves within Year 1, they forfeit their equity.

**What to negotiate:** Accept 4-year vesting (standard and fair). Negotiate for acceleration on change of control — if the company is acquired, remaining vesting accelerates. Also negotiate "good leaver" provisions — if founder steps down for health or personal reasons, vested equity is protected.

#### 8\. Exit Provisions

**What to expect:** Impact investors expect a 7-10 year exit horizon (vs 3-5 years for VCs). Common exits for social enterprises: strategic acquisition by EdTech/HRTech company, secondary sale to larger impact fund, or management buyback.

**What to negotiate:** Ensure no "drag-along" clause that forces a fire sale. Include a right of first refusal for founders if investor wants to exit. Consider a "put option" at Year 8 — investor can sell back shares to company at fair market value. This prevents permanent cap table overhangs.

### Legal Counsel Advisory

Engage a lawyer experienced in social enterprise / impact investing term sheets. Recommended firms: AZB & Partners (Mumbai), Khaitan & Co, Trilegal, or specialist impact law advisory firms like Samvad Partners. Budget ₹3-5 lakhs for term sheet negotiation and shareholder agreement drafting. Never negotiate without independent legal counsel.

### Approach Strategy for CSR Committees

  * **Research the CSR committee:** Annual reports list CSR committee members by name. Identify independent directors with education/research backgrounds.
  * **Use the annual report cycle:** CSR proposals are reviewed Q4 (January-March) for next financial year. Submit proposals by December.
  * **Lead with compliance:** Show exact Schedule VII clause alignment. CSR committees want legal confidence.
  * **Offer talent pipeline quid pro quo:** "Your CSR investment funds PhD career infrastructure, AND gives you privileged access to India's best research talent for R&D; hiring."
  * **Start small:** Propose a ₹25-40L pilot year. Low risk for the corporate, high proof-of-concept value for PhDSetu.
  * **Provide BRSR-ready reporting:** Offer impact data formatted for their Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report. Reduces their reporting burden.
