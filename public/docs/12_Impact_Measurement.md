### The Core Thesis

If we build a trusted, AI-powered career infrastructure platform that connects isolated PhD scholars with curated opportunities, mentors, peers, and skill-building — and fund it through a hybrid model where 10% of recruiter fees support first-generation women researchers — then we will measurably reduce time-to-employment, increase earning potential, decrease mental health distress, and create India's first functioning bridge between doctoral education and productive careers.

**Assumptions to Test:** (1) PhD scholars will trust and adopt a digital career platform if the founder has authentic lived experience. (2) Employers will pay for access to verified, AI-matched PhD talent. (3) Vernacular access significantly increases adoption in tier-2/3 universities. (4) The 10% fee-to-scholarship model is financially sustainable at scale. Each assumption maps to specific validation metrics tracked quarterly.

## Data Collection Methodology

A mixed-methods approach combining quantitative platform analytics with qualitative scholar and employer feedback — designed for rigour without creating survey fatigue.

📊

### Platform Analytics (Automated)

Passive data collection from scholar activity — profile completions, job applications, mentorship bookings, course enrolments, peer connections, session durations, feature adoption rates. Data warehouse updated in real-time. Zero scholar burden.

Real-time Automated 100% coverage

📋

### Scholar Surveys (Structured)

Three-touchpoint survey system: **Intake Survey** (at registration — baseline career awareness, isolation index, mental health score, demographics), **Mid-Year Check-in** (6-month pulse survey), and **Annual Outcome Survey** (comprehensive career and well-being assessment).

3x per year Validated instruments

🎙️

### Follow-Up Interviews (Qualitative)

Stratified sample of 50–100 scholars per quarter for 30-minute semi-structured interviews. Sample balanced by gender, institution tier, discipline, and career outcome. Interviews coded thematically using standardised framework. Stories extracted for impact report narratives.

Quarterly Stratified sample

🏢

### Employer Feedback (360°)

Post-hire employer survey at 30, 90, and 180 days covering: candidate quality vs. expectations, PhD skill relevance, hiring process efficiency, likelihood to re-hire through platform. Quarterly employer NPS and satisfaction benchmarks.

30/90/180 days NPS tracked

### Data Quality Assurance Protocol

Principle | Implementation | Verification  
---|---|---  
**Validity** | Use validated psychometric instruments (PHQ-9 for mental health, UCLA Loneliness Scale adapted for isolation) | Annual instrument review by advisory board  
**Reliability** | Standardised survey administration, interviewer training, inter-rater reliability checks for qualitative coding | Cronbach's alpha > 0.7 for all scales  
**Avoid Double Counting** | Unique scholar IDs linked across all data sources; deduplication in analytics pipeline | Quarterly data audit  
**Attribution** | Control group comparison (waitlist or non-active users), difference-in-differences analysis for career outcomes | External evaluator reviews methodology annually  
**Do No Harm** | IRB-equivalent ethics review for all surveys; data anonymisation; informed consent; right to withdraw | Ethics committee sign-off

## Independent Evaluation Framework

A three-tier evaluation architecture ensuring PhDSetu's impact claims are credible, verifiable, and continuously improving — from internal monitoring to external academic evaluation.

Tier 1 — Internal

### Continuous Monitoring & Learning

Real-time platform analytics and quarterly survey cycles managed by the in-house Impact Measurement team. Dashboard-driven, focused on operational improvement and early detection of impact gaps.

  * • **Frequency:** Continuous (analytics) + Quarterly (surveys)
  * • **Team:** Impact Measurement Lead + Data Analyst
  * • **Output:** Quarterly Impact Brief (internal), Dashboard updates
  * • **Audience:** Leadership team, Board Impact Committee



Tier 2 — External Audit

### Annual Independent Assessment

Third-party evaluation by a credible impact assessment firm or academic institution. Reviews methodology, validates data, tests attribution claims, and provides improvement recommendations.

  * • **Frequency:** Annual (aligned with impact report cycle)
  * • **Evaluator:** Rotated every 3 years to prevent familiarity bias
  * • **Output:** Independent Evaluator's Statement (published in annual report)
  * • **Audience:** CSR funders, government partners, public



Tier 3 — Participatory

### Scholar & Stakeholder Voice

Evaluation that centres the lived experience of scholars themselves. Annual scholar feedback panel, employer advisory council review, and community-driven impact definition exercises.

  * • **Frequency:** Annual Scholar Impact Panel + Biannual Employer Council
  * • **Method:** Focus groups, participatory ranking, Most Significant Change technique
  * • **Output:** Stakeholder Voice Report (appendix to annual report)
  * • **Audience:** Board, programme design team, scholar community



### Evaluation Calendar

Quarter | Internal Activities | External Activities | Key Deliverables  
---|---|---|---  
**Q1 (Apr–Jun)** | Annual baseline survey launch; dashboard reset; previous year data close | Independent evaluator engagement; TOR finalised | Annual Impact Report (previous year) published  
**Q2 (Jul–Sep)** | Mid-cycle pulse survey; scholar outcome tracking; funder interim reports | Evaluator field work — data validation, scholar interviews | Quarterly Impact Brief; CSR funder interim reports  
**Q3 (Oct–Dec)** | Scholar Impact Panel facilitated; employer satisfaction survey round | Evaluator draft findings shared; management response prepared | Stakeholder Voice Report; Employer Satisfaction Report  
**Q4 (Jan–Mar)** | Annual outcome survey; data aggregation; story collection for report | Final evaluation report received; findings integrated into strategy | Draft Annual Impact Report; Board strategy review  
  
### Evaluation Principles

  * **Independence:** External evaluator has full access to data and scholars; no editorial control by PhDSetu
  * **Transparency:** Evaluation methodology, findings, and management responses published in full
  * **Utility:** Findings feed directly into programme design improvements — evaluation is not performative
  * **Proportionality:** Evaluation rigour scaled to programme maturity and funder requirements
  * **Do No Harm:** Scholar participation in evaluation is voluntary; data is anonymised in all published reports
  * **Learning Orientation:** Negative findings are treated as opportunities; no suppression of unfavourable data
