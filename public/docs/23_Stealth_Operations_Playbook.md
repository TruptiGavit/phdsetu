## Legal Risk Assessment (India-Specific)

A thorough analysis of the legal landscape governing moonlighting, side ventures, and employment obligations under Indian law — including key case law that overwhelmingly favors founders. 

### Employment Contract Clause Analysis

Clause Type | What It Says | Legal Enforceability | Risk Level | Your Position  
---|---|---|---|---  
**IP Assignment** | All work product created during employment belongs to employer | Enforceable only for work done using company resources, time, or related to company's business | Medium | PhDSetu is an education/career platform — not related to chemical engineering, sociology departments, or employer's core business  
**Non-Compete** | Cannot work for competitor or start competing business during/after employment | Unenforceable post-termination under Indian law (Section 27) | Low | Section 27 of Indian Contract Act 1872 voids post-employment non-competes entirely  
**Moonlighting / Exclusivity** | Employee shall devote full time and attention exclusively to employer | Enforceable during employment hours; cannot restrict what you do on personal time | Medium | All PhDSetu work done on personal time, personal devices, personal networks  
**Conflict of Interest** | Cannot engage in activities that conflict with employer's interests | Enforceable only if actual conflict exists | Low | PhDSetu serves PhD scholars in career transitions — no overlap with any employer's business  
**Disclosure Obligation** | Must inform employer of any outside business interests | Enforceable — but only if explicitly stated in contract | Medium | Check each founder's contract; if present, time disclosure strategically during Phase 2  
**Fiduciary Duty** | Senior employees owe duty of loyalty to employer | Enforceable — but limited to not actively harming employer's business | Low | PhDSetu doesn't compete with, harm, or divert resources from any employer  
  
### Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872

_"Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void."_  
  
This is the single most important legal protection for PhDSetu's founders. Indian courts have consistently held that post-employment non-compete clauses are unenforceable. Even during employment, restrictions must be reasonable and directly related to protecting legitimate business interests — not blanket prohibitions on all outside activity. 

### Key Case Law References

Case | Court | Year | Key Holding | Relevance to PhDSetu  
---|---|---|---|---  
**Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning & Manufacturing Co.** | Supreme Court of India | 1967 | Restraint of trade clauses during employment are valid only if reasonable and necessary to protect employer's legitimate interests. Post-employment restraints are void under Section 27. | Establishes that a non-compete clause must pass a reasonableness test — PhDSetu has zero business overlap with any employer  
**Pepsi Foods Ltd. v. Bharat Coca-Cola Holdings** | Delhi High Court | 1999 | Post-termination non-compete agreements are void under Section 27. Employee cannot be prevented from using their general skills and knowledge. | Confirms founders can use their general expertise (chemistry, sociology, AI) in any venture after hours  
**Wipro Ltd. v. Beckman Coulter International** | Delhi High Court | 2006 | Non-solicitation and non-compete clauses must be narrowly tailored. Broad restrictions are void. | Even large companies cannot enforce broad non-compete — PhDSetu is a completely different domain  
**Superintendence Company of India v. Krishan Murgai** | Supreme Court of India | 1981 | An agreement restraining an employee from taking up any employment after leaving is void under Section 27. | Supreme Court precedent that blanket employment restrictions are unenforceable  
**FL Smidth Pvt. Ltd. v. Secan Invescast (India) Pvt. Ltd.** | Madras High Court | 2013 | Restraint during employment period can be valid if reasonable, but cannot extend beyond employment. | During-employment restrictions require reasonableness — no employer has a PhD career platform business

### Government vs. Private Sector Differences

#### Government / PSU Employees

  * CCS (Conduct) Rules explicitly prohibit government servants from engaging in business without permission
  * Rule 15: No government servant shall engage in any trade or business or undertake any employment
  * Violation can lead to disciplinary proceedings
  * Prior permission mechanism exists — but rarely granted
  * **Mitigation:** If Sonali's prior role was BARC (DAE), current private sector role has different rules
  * Academic institutions (for Radha) have their own conduct rules — check university/institution bylaws



#### Private Sector Employees

  * No statutory prohibition on moonlighting — governed only by employment contract
  * Section 27 protection is strongest here
  * Most contracts have standard non-compete boilerplate — largely unenforceable
  * Key risk is breach of confidentiality or IP assignment clauses (mitigated by complete domain separation)
  * Worst realistic outcome: termination of employment (not criminal liability)
  * Many IT/corporate employees openly run side ventures — increasingly accepted culturally



**When Moonlighting Crosses the Line** Moonlighting becomes legally problematic when: (1) you use employer resources (laptop, IP, data, contacts) for side work, (2) your side venture directly competes with your employer, (3) you divert employer's business opportunities to your venture, (4) you work on your venture during official work hours, or (5) your performance at your primary job measurably declines. PhDSetu violates none of these if proper separation is maintained.

## Employment Contract Audit Checklist

Each founder must audit their employment contract and offer letter line by line. Use this checklist to identify risk areas and plan mitigations. 

### Clauses to Look For

Clause | Example Language | Red Flag? | What To Do  
---|---|---|---  
**Exclusivity / Full-Time Devotion** | "The Employee shall devote their full working time, attention, and abilities to the business of the Company" | Moderate | "Full working time" protects you — PhDSetu is done on personal time. Keep clear time boundaries.  
**Blanket IP Assignment** | "All inventions, developments, and work product created by Employee during the term of employment shall be the sole property of the Company" | High | Legally enforceable only for work related to employer's business or done on company time/resources. Document that PhDSetu work uses only personal resources.  
**Work-for-Hire with Broad Scope** | "Including but not limited to inventions conceived outside of regular working hours" | High | Indian courts have limited such broad clauses. Get a legal opinion letter if this exists in your contract.  
**Non-Compete (During Employment)** | "Employee shall not engage in any business that competes with the Company during employment" | Low | PhDSetu does not compete with any employer's business. No overlap in domain.  
**Disclosure of Outside Interests** | "Employee must promptly disclose any outside business interest or directorship" | Moderate | If present, plan disclosure timing carefully during Phase 2. May use Section 8 entity structure to delay visibility.  
**Non-Solicitation of Colleagues** | "Employee shall not solicit or recruit other employees of the Company" | Low | PhDSetu does not target any employer's workforce. No risk here.  
**Confidentiality / NDA** | "Employee shall not disclose any confidential information of the Company to any third party" | Low | PhDSetu has no use for any employer's confidential information. Maintain clean separation.  
**Moonlighting Policy (Separate HR Document)** | "Employees must seek prior written approval before engaging in any outside employment or business" | Moderate | Check if this is in the contract or just an HR policy. Policies are weaker than contractual obligations. Plan timing of disclosure accordingly.

**Green Flag Indicators** Your contract is founder-friendly if: (1) exclusivity mentions only "working hours" or "office hours," (2) IP assignment is limited to "work related to the Company's business," (3) no non-compete clause exists, or it's limited to 6 months post-employment, (4) no mandatory disclosure of outside interests, (5) no separate moonlighting policy, and (6) the company culture is known to be startup-friendly. 

**Red Flag Indicators — Seek Legal Counsel** Get a lawyer immediately if: (1) IP clause covers "all inventions conceived during employment period regardless of time or resources used," (2) there's a specific prohibition on holding directorships in any other company, (3) your employer is in the education or career services space (potential overlap argument), (4) you signed a separate moonlighting prohibition document, or (5) your employment is governed by specific industry regulations (banking, defense, etc.).

### Using Nominees as Initial Directors

#### Pros

  * Founders' names don't appear on MCA records during stealth
  * Family members (spouse, parent, sibling) can be initial directors legally
  * Common practice for founders in sensitive employment situations
  * Can be restructured later when founders go public
  * DIN numbers are publicly searchable — nominees shield you



#### Cons & Risks

  * Nominee may incur legal liability as a director
  * Requires absolute trust — nominee has legal control
  * Board resolutions and compliance fall on nominee's name
  * Tax implications for nominee if they receive director remuneration
  * Must sign proper nominee arrangement (not always enforceable under Indian law)



**DIN & MCA Visibility** The Director Identification Number (DIN) is a unique number assigned by MCA to every director. DIN records are publicly searchable on the MCA portal (mca.gov.in). Anyone can search by DIN or name and find all companies where a person is a director. This is why using nominees during Phase 1 is critical — your name should not appear on any MCA filing until you're ready for Phase 2/3. However, even nominees' details are public. Choose nominees whose connection to you is not immediately obvious to casual searchers (e.g., a spouse with a different surname, or a trusted friend).

### Clean Separation Doctrine

### The 6 Pillars of Clean Separation

Maintain these consistently to have an ironclad defense if any employer ever challenges your PhDSetu involvement:

**1\. Time Separation** All PhDSetu work done outside of office hours. Maintain a simple time log (even rough) showing you worked evenings and weekends only.

**2\. Device Separation** Never use employer's laptop, phone, or any equipment for PhDSetu. Dedicated personal devices only.

**3\. Network Separation** Never use office WiFi, VPN, or any employer network for PhDSetu activity.

**4\. IP Separation** No employer data, contacts, research, or proprietary information used in PhDSetu.

**5\. Resource Separation** Personal funds only. No employer reimbursements, expense accounts, or resources diverted.

**6\. Domain Separation** PhDSetu's business (PhD career services) has zero overlap with employer's business domain.

### What If a Colleague Finds PhDSetu?

#### Colleague sees the PhDSetu LinkedIn page or website

Medium Risk

  1. Stay calm. If they mention it casually, don't react defensively.
  2. If they ask "Are you involved?" — respond: "Oh interesting, I think I've seen that page. It's about PhD careers, right?" Keep it vague and redirect.
  3. If they press further — "I know someone who's involved, it's a social impact initiative for PhD scholars." Frame it as distant knowledge.
  4. Do NOT confirm or deny involvement directly. Any confirmation creates a witness.
  5. After the conversation, assess whether this colleague is likely to escalate. If they're a friend, the risk is low. If they're a competitive colleague or close to management, increase OpSec vigilance.
  6. Document the interaction in your stealth log (date, who, what was said, your response).

### Tax Implications

**Important Tax Considerations** (1) Any income earned from PhDSetu must be declared in your personal ITR (even if small). (2) If PhDSetu is a Section 8 company, any salary/stipend drawn by founders is taxable as salary income. (3) CSR grants received by a Section 8 entity are not taxable as income to the entity. (4) Keep all PhDSetu financial records separate from personal finances — your CA will thank you. (5) If you're a director (even nominee), your name appears in the entity's ITR filing. ### CSR Fund Applications

  * **Large corporates may due-diligence directors:** This is where nominee structure may raise questions. Consider timing CSR applications for Phase 2 when some founder info can be shared under NDA.

## Risk Scenarios & Response Playbook

Detailed response plans for every conceivable scenario that could expose founders or create conflict during the stealth phase. Memorize these. Practice them. 

**Golden Rule For All Scenarios** Never panic. Never lie outright (it always makes things worse). Never confirm more than necessary. Deflect, reframe, and buy time. Consult your lawyer before making any written statement to your employer. 

#### Scenario 1: Boss Directly Asks "Are You Running PhDSetu?"

Severity: HIGH

  1. **Don't panic or deny outright.** Take a breath. "That's an interesting question — what made you ask?"
  2. **Assess what they know.** Let them talk first. Their level of knowledge determines your response.
  3. **If they have no evidence:** "I'm aware of it — it's a social initiative for PhD scholars that some IIT alumni are working on. I've offered some informal advice." (positions you as a peripheral supporter, not a founder)
  4. **If they have solid evidence:** "I've been involved in a social impact initiative supporting PhD career transitions. It's done entirely on my personal time with personal resources, and it has zero overlap with our work here. I should have mentioned it earlier — I was waiting for the right time."
  5. **Immediately pivot to reassurance:** "My commitment to [company] is unchanged. My work here hasn't been affected — and I'll make sure it never is."
  6. **If they demand a formal meeting:** "Of course. Can I have a day to prepare some context? I want to make sure I give you a complete picture." Use this time to consult your lawyer.
  7. **Follow up in writing** (after lawyer consultation) with a brief email confirming the conversation and your position.



#### Scenario 2: Colleague Sees Your WhatsApp Community

Severity: MEDIUM

  1. **This is why you use a separate phone/SIM.** If they see it on your phone, it should be your PhDSetu phone, not your personal/work phone.
  2. **If they mention it casually:** "Oh yeah, it's a WhatsApp community for PhD scholars. I know someone who runs it — they asked me to help moderate. It's a volunteer thing."
  3. **Minimize your visible role:** Ensure you're not the only admin. Having multiple admins makes your involvement look like one of many.
  4. **If they want to join:** Let them! It's a public community. Their joining doesn't expose your founder role if you're using first-name-only admin identity.
  5. **Post-incident:** Review your WhatsApp OpSec. Is your admin name identifiable? Is your personal phone number visible? Tighten up.



#### Scenario 3: HR Sends a Company-Wide Moonlighting Policy Email

Severity: MEDIUM

  1. **Read the policy carefully.** Distinguish between: (a) informational/awareness, (b) requiring disclosure, (c) prohibiting all outside work.
  2. **Do NOT rush to disclose.** A general policy email doesn't mean they know about you specifically. It may be triggered by industry trends (post-COVID moonlighting debates).
  3. **If it requires disclosure by a deadline:** Consult your lawyer immediately. You may need to: (a) disclose PhDSetu (beginning Phase 2 earlier than planned), or (b) restructure your involvement temporarily (step down as active operator, let other founders handle).
  4. **If it prohibits all outside work:** Check legal enforceability (likely weak under Section 27). However, compliance may be politically wise. Consult lawyer on how to respond.
  5. **Don't discuss the policy with colleagues** in a way that reveals your personal concern about it.
  6. **Document everything:** Save the email, note the date, record your assessment.



#### Scenario 4: LinkedIn Algorithm Shows PhDSetu to Work Connections

Severity: MEDIUM-LOW

  1. **This happens when:** PhDSetu's page is followed by people in your network, or LinkedIn detects location/industry overlap.
  2. **Prevention:** During Phase 1, don't follow/like/engage with PhDSetu from personal LinkedIn. Don't use the same device/IP for personal LinkedIn and PhDSetu page management.
  3. **If a colleague says "I saw this PhDSetu page":** "Oh cool, what is it?" Play ignorant. LinkedIn shows lots of random content.
  4. **If they specifically connect it to you:** "Interesting — I don't think I'm connected to them. Maybe LinkedIn is suggesting it because of our PhD/IIT connections."
  5. **Post-incident:** Review PhDSetu page admin settings. Ensure you're not logged into your personal LinkedIn when managing the page.



#### Scenario 5: A Journalist Wants to Profile the Founders

Severity: MEDIUM

  1. **This is actually positive news** — it means PhDSetu is gaining traction. Control the narrative.
  2. **Respond promptly but redirect:** "We're thrilled by your interest! We'd love to share PhDSetu's mission, impact, and vision. Our founding team prefers to keep a low profile for now — we'll be doing founder profiles when we formally launch."
  3. **Offer alternatives:** Impact data, community testimonials, industry analysis, expert quotes (anonymous).
  4. **If they insist on founder names:** "We understand, but our team members are still in their current professional roles and would prefer to not be profiled individually at this stage. We can share anonymized backgrounds — 'PhD IIT Bombay, 20+ years industry' — for credibility."
  5. **Consider doing a written Q &A;** rather than live interview — gives you control over every word.
  6. **Get final copy approval** if possible — many journalists will share quotes for accuracy.



#### Scenario 6: An Investor Wants a Meeting During Work Hours

Severity: LOW-MEDIUM

  1. **Schedule for evening/weekend:** "We'd love to meet! Our team is available [Saturday morning / weekday evening after 7 PM]. Would that work?"
  2. **If they insist on weekday daytime:** Take a half-day personal leave. Frame it as "personal appointment" — no details needed.
  3. **For initial meetings:** Suggest a video call first (easier to schedule outside work hours) before committing to an in-person meeting.
  4. **Location:** Never meet at a venue near your office or where colleagues frequent. Choose a neutral location in a different part of the city.
  5. **If investor asks about your employment:** "All three co-founders are currently building PhDSetu alongside our professional commitments. Our plan is to transition to full-time as we hit [specific milestones]. Here's our transition roadmap..." This is actually common and investors understand it.



#### Scenario 7: Your Employer Starts a Competing Initiative

Severity: HIGH

  1. **Assess the overlap carefully.** "Competing" could mean: (a) a general CSR/education initiative (low overlap), (b) a PhD hiring program (some overlap), or (c) a career platform for researchers (high overlap).
  2. **If overlap is minimal:** Continue as planned but document the differences meticulously. PhDSetu serves PhD scholars broadly; employer initiative likely serves their specific hiring needs.
  3. **If overlap is significant:** Consult your lawyer immediately. You may need to: (a) recuse yourself from the employer's initiative, (b) accelerate your Phase 2 disclosure, or (c) in extreme cases, reassess your involvement in PhDSetu.
  4. **Never volunteer** for or participate in your employer's competing initiative — it creates an actual conflict of interest.
  5. **Document the timeline:** Show that PhDSetu predates the employer's initiative — this matters if IP disputes arise.



#### Scenario 8: Someone Outs You on Social Media

Severity: HIGH

  1. **Don't engage publicly.** Do not reply to the post, do not share it, do not draw more attention to it.
  2. **Screenshot and document everything** — the original post, comments, shares, timestamp.
  3. **Contact the person privately:** "Hey, I noticed your post. I'd appreciate if you could remove or edit the mention of my name. I'm working on this initiative quietly for now and would prefer to announce my involvement on my own terms."
  4. **If they refuse or it's already viral:** Shift to Phase 2 crisis mode. Prepare your employer conversation. Consult your lawyer.
  5. **If your employer sees it:** Deploy Scenario 1 response (boss confrontation plan).
  6. **Post-crisis:** Audit who knows about your involvement. Tighten the circle. Remind all who know to maintain confidentiality.

### Key Indian Case Law Summaries

### Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning (1967)

**Court:** Supreme Court of India

**Holding:** Non-compete restrictions during employment are valid only if reasonable and protect legitimate business interests. Post-employment restraints are void under Section 27.

**Takeaway:** The "reasonableness" test protects PhDSetu founders — there's no reasonable business interest of any employer in blocking a PhD career platform.

### Pepsi Foods v. Bharat Coca-Cola (1999)

**Court:** Delhi High Court

**Holding:** Post-termination non-competes are void. Employees cannot be restrained from using general skills, knowledge, and industry experience.

**Takeaway:** Founders' expertise in their fields (chemistry, sociology, AI) is their own — no employer can claim it.

### Superintendence Co. v. Krishan Murgai (1981)

**Court:** Supreme Court of India

**Holding:** Agreements preventing ex-employees from any employment are void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act.

**Takeaway:** Even the broadest non-compete clauses crumble under Section 27. Founders are legally free.

### Wipro v. Beckman Coulter (2006)

**Court:** Delhi High Court

**Holding:** Non-solicitation and non-compete clauses must be narrowly tailored to specific business interests. Broad restrictions are unenforceable.

**Takeaway:** Employers must show specific harm — which is impossible when PhDSetu and their businesses are in completely different domains.
