Brainstorm 9

## Inclusion, Accessibility & Expansion

How can PhDSetu start focused without becoming structurally narrow? Start with Indian STEM PhD/M.Tech researchers, but the underlying system should eventually work for everyone — from IIT scholars to state-university scholars, humanities PhDs, researchers with disabilities, and international researchers.

### 🎯 The Big Question

How can PhDSetu start focused without becoming structurally narrow?

The content changes. The fundamental framework doesn't:

Research→ Capability→ Possibility→ Evidence→ Action

🎓

### Launch Market

Indian STEM PhD/M.Tech researchers — manageable, focused launch market with clear need and reachable institutions.

🌍

### Eventual Reach

IIT scholar → state-university scholar → humanities PhD → researcher with disability → international researcher. Same framework, expanding content.

### 🪜 Distribution Ladder (Point 4)

Flagship workshop → Campus Setu → Mini-workshop kit → Faculty/placement coordinator → Virtual regional session → PhDSetu cohort

1

Tier-2/Tier-3 should NOT be a later expansion

  * They should be part of the initial design
  * The difference may be access to opportunity, not necessarily capability
  * A researcher at a less-known university may have: excellent experimental skills, publications, independent problem-solving experience, instrumentation expertise, strong persistence, deep technical capability
  * But may have less access to: industry exposure, career services, alumni networks, recruiters, career language, mock interviews, professional networks
  * That is exactly the gap PhDSetu can reduce



2

Don't pretend institution reputation doesn't matter

  * Sometimes it does. Brand can influence: shortlisting, campus recruitment, network access, initial assumptions, recruiter visibility
  * But the useful question is: Which parts of the hiring equation can you control?
  * There are many: capability articulation, evidence, professional communication, role understanding, company research, networking, portfolio, interview performance, skill-gap reduction
  * That's a stronger message



3

Build "visibility infrastructure"

  * Premier institutions already provide some visibility automatically. Others don't.
  * Eventually a scholar profile could show: Research problems, Capabilities, Techniques, Evidence, Career interests, Publications, Career experiments
  * Rather than leading primarily with: University name
  * This could become important later on the employer side too



4

Tier-2/3 workshop strategy

  * Don't require personal visits to hundreds of universities
  * Build a distribution ladder: Flagship workshop → Campus Setu → Mini-workshop kit → Faculty/placement coordinator → Virtual regional session → PhDSetu cohort
  * A student from IIT Ropar might run the exercise at their home institution
  * A professor could run it with research scholars
  * A Campus Setu could facilitate it
  * Creates reach without huge cost



5

Low-bandwidth matters

  * If serious about wider India, shouldn't assume: fast laptop, large monitor, constant Wi-Fi, high-end phone
  * Design: mobile-first, fast-loading, compressed videos, transcripts, downloadable worksheets, audio where useful, save-and-resume, minimal unnecessary animation
  * The Career Map especially should have a usable list/text alternative
  * A beautiful visualization that fails on a low-end phone isn't inclusive



6

English confidence vs professional capability

  * Someone may be an excellent researcher and less fluent in spoken English
  * Shouldn't equate: English polish = intelligence
  * But English communication remains practically relevant in many industry environments
  * PhDSetu should approach this as a developable professional capability, not a judgment
  * "Here's how to structure your answer clearly" rather than "Speak sophisticated English"
  * Clarity > vocabulary



7

Multilingual support (staged approach)

  * Stage 1: English platform. Plain, accessible English. Avoid unnecessary corporate jargon.
  * Stage 2: Key career explainers in selected Indian languages (Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Bengali etc.) based on actual user demand
  * Stage 3: Multilingual interface where usage justifies it. But preserve professional terminology.
  * Example: Root Cause Analysis can be explained in Hindi while still teaching the English industry term



8

Accessibility for disabled researchers — treat as product requirement, not special project

  * Visual: Screen-reader compatibility, Semantic headings, Alt text, High contrast, Keyboard navigation, Scalable text, Don't communicate meaning only through color
  * Hearing: Captions, Transcripts, Written exercise instructions, Live-event captioning where feasible
  * Mobility: Keyboard-only navigation, Large interaction targets, No interactions requiring precise mouse movement, Physical workshop venue accessibility
  * Cognitive/learning: Clear layouts, Plain language, Short sections, Consistent navigation, Ability to pause/resume, Downloadable summaries
  * This benefits everyone



9

The Career Map creates a specific accessibility challenge

  * A graph can be difficult for: screen readers, small screens, motor accessibility, cognitive overload
  * Every visual graph should have an equivalent "Explore as list"
  * Example: Your capability: Experimental Design → Potential domains: Materials R&D, Product Development, Process Development, Applications, Quality/Reliability → Then drill down
  * No critical information should exist only in the visual network



10

Workshop accessibility

  * Slides: large text, high contrast, limited text, avoid tiny data labels, verbally explain important visuals
  * Videos: captions
  * Exercises: written instructions as well as spoken
  * QR: also provide a short navigable route because QR shouldn't be the only access method
  * Microphones: use them even if you think your voice carries
  * Audience questions: repeat the question before answering
  * Small changes with significant benefits



11

Hearing-impaired students

  * Build toward: captioned workshop videos, transcripts, written toolkits, live captioning where venue technology permits, visual exercise instructions
  * Later: selected Indian Sign Language career modules could be very meaningful
  * Part of the roadmap, not immediate promise



12

Accessibility becomes commercially useful too

  * Institutions increasingly care about accessible learning
  * If PhDSetu can say: "All core learning resources include captions, transcripts and accessible alternatives" — that's valuable for university partnerships



13

Humanities and social sciences — testing whether the philosophy is strong

  * PhD in History: Old career thinking (Professor, Researcher, Archivist)
  * Capability mapping reveals: archival research, source verification, evidence synthesis, long-form writing, argument construction, qualitative analysis, interviewing, teaching, public communication
  * Possibilities expand: policy research, publishing, think tanks, museums, knowledge management, research consulting, communications, content strategy, UX research, public history, cultural organizations
  * The PhDSetu model survives



14

Literature PhD example

  * Capabilities: deep textual analysis, interpretation, writing, argumentation, synthesis, teaching, editing, cultural analysis
  * Adjacencies: publishing, communications, editorial, learning design, content strategy, research, policy communication, brand/content, cultural organizations
  * degree → capability → adjacency



15

Social Sciences — potentially even easier

  * PhD in Psychology/Sociology/Anthropology etc.
  * Capabilities: qualitative research, survey design, interviews, statistics, behaviour analysis, field research, synthesis
  * Adjacencies: UX research, consumer insights, market research, policy, people analytics, organizational research, consulting, behavioural science



16

Arts — more challenging but possible

  * Fine Arts research: creative practice, visual communication, cultural research, project development, critique, curation, audience understanding
  * Potential paths: creative industries, curation, design research, education, cultural organizations, communications, experience design
  * Should bring experts from these domains before pretending STEM-derived frameworks automatically map perfectly



17

Don't expand by simply adding career pages

  * Don't do: "Add 50 humanities jobs"
  * Instead recruit domain contributors
  * Ask: What does research look like in your discipline? What capabilities does it create? What transitions actually happen?
  * Then extend the capability graph. Much more robust.



18

PhDSetu contributor network — another future community layer

  * Domain Stewards / Expert Contributors for: Life Sciences, Materials, Computer Science, Social Sciences, Humanities, Policy, Arts
  * Help validate: career paths, capability relationships, transition stories, terminology
  * Protects content quality as expansion goes beyond initial expertise



19

Gender inclusion

  * Career decisions can involve practical questions around: location, travel, shift work, lab safety, parental leave, flexibility, return after career breaks, workplace culture
  * PhDSetu can help scholars ask intelligent questions about work environments without prescribing personal choices
  * Possible questions: Are there women in technical leadership? What does progression look like? What flexibility policies actually exist? Does this role involve extensive travel or shift work?



20

Career breaks

  * Researchers can have: maternity/paternity breaks, caregiving responsibilities, health-related breaks, visa delays, family transitions, industry-academia gaps
  * PhDSetu should normalize explaining career gaps professionally without forcing disclosure of private details



21

Geographic inclusion

  * India isn't just Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai, Delhi NCR
  * Researchers may want to remain in: Chandigarh, Ahmedabad, Vadodara, Chennai, Kolkata, Kochi, Indore, Bhubaneswar, or smaller cities
  * Career exploration should eventually support: What research careers exist in locations I'm willing to consider?
  * First: possibility. Then: constraints.



22

Global expansion

  * Core philosophy is global, but career ecosystem isn't
  * PhD-to-industry transition differs across India, Germany, US, UK, Singapore
  * Differences include: job titles, visa, postdoc norms, salary structures, hiring cycles, industry clusters, doctoral training, career offices
  * Global expansion should use: Global framework + local career intelligence



23

Potential expansion sequence

  * Phase 1: India — STEM + M.Tech
  * Phase 2: India — broader sciences + social sciences
  * Phase 3: India — humanities/arts with expert partners
  * Phase 4: Indian researchers exploring global careers (natural bridge)
  * Phase 5: International researcher cohorts (start with regions where Indian researchers have strong networks)
  * Phase 6: Localized international PhDSetu ecosystems



24

Indian researchers abroad could be a bridge

  * Many Indian PhDs/postdocs in US, UK, Germany, Netherlands, Singapore, Australia, Switzerland
  * Their transition stories teach: international careers, industry differences, postdoc decisions, visa realities, return-to-India careers
  * They can also become mentors/contributors
  * Global expansion can begin through the existing Indian research diaspora



25

"Return to India" could eventually be a useful career path

  * For researchers abroad: "What opportunities exist if I return?"
  * PhDSetu could connect: research capability, Indian R&D sectors, companies, labs, startups, career stories
  * Could become a distinctive vertical later



26

Socioeconomic accessibility

  * Protect against: best tools = paid
  * Basic career discovery and essential tools should remain accessible
  * Advanced guided support can be paid
  * Institutions/employers can subsidize access



27

Accessibility should become part of the product definition

  * Not: PhDSetu + Accessibility (later)
  * But: PhDSetu = accessible researcher career navigation
  * Same with tier-2/3: Not PhDSetu + Tier-2 initiative, but PhDSetu was built for researchers irrespective of institute brand
  * Stronger identity



28

Be careful with "India's first" claim

  * Before using publicly, verify competitive landscape carefully
  * "First" claims are easy to challenge
  * Safer positioning: "A career navigation platform built specifically for researchers"
  * Research competitors later and determine if claim is defensible



29

Inclusivity also changes stories

  * Don't show only: IIT → Google, IISc → global pharma
  * Deliberately feature: regional university → R&D, state university → consulting, tier-3 institution → application science, career break → industry, PhD → unexpected function, failed transition → second attempt, academia → industry, industry → academia, STEM → adjacent non-lab role



30

The success story problem

  * Career platforms often publish shallow: "She got a job at Company X!"
  * PhDSetu success story should say: What did she learn about her capabilities? What career did she discover? What gap did she identify? What experiment did she run? What changed?
  * Sometimes success might be: "I realized consulting wasn't for me." — That's also valuable.
  * Career navigation isn't only about offers. It's about better decisions.



31

Inclusion Test — before launching a feature, ask:

  * ACCESS: Can someone on a phone and slower connection use it?
  * ABILITY: Can someone using assistive technology use it?
  * LANGUAGE: Is the wording unnecessarily complicated?
  * INSTITUTION: Does it assume elite-university resources?
  * DISCIPLINE: Does it assume laboratory/STEM research?
  * MONEY: Is essential value available without payment?
  * GEOGRAPHY: Does it assume a metro-city career?



32

An important PhDSetu principle emerges

  * Equal capability does not always mean equal access to opportunity
  * PhDSetu can't remove every structural difference
  * But it can improve: career information, career language, visibility, preparation, professional connections, evidence articulation, access to role models
  * That's a realistic mission

Would NOT lead with "India's first"

What category should PhDSetu own?

  * Career Navigation for Researchers
  * NOT: Career counselling, PhD jobs, Placement portal, EdTech, Mentorship marketplace, Professional network
  * PhDSetu helps researchers navigate from research experience to career possibilities
  * Broad enough for long term, specific enough to understand

The positioning statement

  * Homepage shouldn't contain that whole sentence



4

User-facing proposition

  * Strongest question: "Where could your research take you?"
  * Then: "Discover careers through your capabilities—not only your degree."
  * CTA: "Explore your possibilities"

The strongest brand belief

7

What does "Setu" mean strategically?

  * Setu = bridge
  * Not simply: Academia → Industry (too narrow, some may stay in academia, government, policy, consulting, startups, scientific communication, IP, entrepreneurship)
  * The bridge is: Research → Possibility, or Research → Career
  * More specifically: What I researched → What I can do → Where that can matter

Potential tagline territory

  * Direction B — Navigation: "Navigate beyond your degree."

  * Brand: PhDSetu

9

What should PhDSetu sound like?

  * Not corporate, not academic, not "life coach", not recruiter
  * Should feel like: a smart senior researcher sitting beside you with a whiteboard
  * Curious, Evidence-driven, Practical, Warm, Occasionally provocative
  * Comfortable saying: "We don't know yet—let's test it."



10

Use research language intelligently

  * Career decision → Career hypothesis
  * Try networking → Run a career experiment
  * You have soft skills → Where is the evidence?
  * You might like this career → Let's investigate the adjacency
  * Career failure → What did the data tell us?
  * Creates distinctive brand vocabulary



11

Don't overdo scientific metaphors

  * Not every sentence should become: "Catalyze your career trajectory through evidence-driven hypothesis optimization"
  * Use research language where it makes complex career ideas simpler, not as branding decoration



12

PhDSetu's vocabulary — intentionally establish proprietary terms

  * Capability Map, Career Adjacency, Evidence Bank, Career Experiment, Research Pitch, JD Decoder, Company Fundamentals, Career Map
  * Users should say: "I did my Capability Map", "I'm running a Career Experiment"
  * Methodology becomes brand



13

Competitive map — what users currently substitute for PhDSetu

  * LinkedIn: provides people, jobs, content but doesn't systematically translate research → capabilities → adjacencies
  * Job boards: answer "What's open?" vs PhDSetu answers "What should I even be looking for?" — fundamental distinction
  * University placement cells: BITS Pilani has dedicated PhD placement/career initiative. PhDSetu can complement with reusable navigation methodology and cross-institution ecosystem
  * Industry-academia portals: national portal connects PhD scholars with industry research projects, sponsorship, collaboration — useful but different from career-navigation
  * Skills/industry-connect platforms: Skill-Grid covers students, researchers, faculty, universities, companies. Shows why PhDSetu should remain narrower and deeper
  * Academic opportunity platforms: ServiceSetu offers academic jobs, calls for papers, postdocs, workshops. Opportunity aggregation isn't central territory
  * Workshops/programs: INYAS PRAYOJAN helps postdocs/early-career researchers. "We run career workshops for PhDs" isn't differentiation
  * Career-transition tools: IndustryCareerGuide targets STEM PhDs/scientists, uses assessments, personalized plans. Need deeper competitive analysis here



14

The whitespace

  * Not simply "CAREERS FOR PhDs"
  * But: CAPABILITY-BASED CAREER NAVIGATION FOR RESEARCHERS
  * Specifically: Decode research → Identify capabilities → Explore adjacencies → Validate with real transitions → Build evidence → Run career experiments → Take action
  * Much harder to confuse with a job board

Career Map becomes central to the brand

  * Visual embodiment of the brand



16

"Decode My Research" could become the acquisition hook

  * "What did your PhD actually teach you?" → Paste 3-5 sentences about research → Capabilities → Adjacent problem spaces → Possible roles → "Explore these on PhDSetu"
  * Important caveat: AI suggestions are hypotheses to investigate, not declarations. Epistemic humility should become part of the brand.

Don't promise jobs

Brainstorm 11

## Master Roadmap, Metrics & IIT Ropar Execution Plan

From foundation freeze to 12-month ambition — a backward-planned execution roadmap with clear priorities, metrics, and internal rules for the IIT Ropar pilot.

### H1 — Problem

Researchers genuinely struggle to see career possibilities beyond their degree/thesis

### H2 — Method

Capability Mapping + Career Adjacency changes how they think about careers

### H3 — Product

They find enough value in PhDSetu to continue exploring after the workshop

### H4 — Behaviour

Some students actually take career actions afterward

### H5 — Network

Some students are willing to help other researchers navigate the same problem

### Core Journey

QR → EXPLORE → SIGN UP → SAVE → ACT → RETURN

### 🎯 Launch Priorities — P0 / P1 / P2

### P0 — Absolutely Required Before IIT Ropar

  * **PRODUCT:** Reliable signup/login, Persistent user profile, Minimal onboarding, Mobile-responsive workshop landing page, Working Career Map, Working Career Path pages, Workshop registration persistence, Working community posting/comments, Basic toolkit/resource access, Privacy controls, Basic analytics, Basic error monitoring
  * **WORKSHOP:** Final 60/90/120-minute architecture, Complete deck, Speaker notes, Interactive exercises, Capability Map worksheet, Career Adjacency exercise, Evidence Bank worksheet, Company Fundamentals checklist, 7-Day Career Experiment, QR codes tested, Backup offline materials
  * **CONTENT:** Several high-quality Career Paths, 5-10 excellent researcher transition stories, Seed community discussions, Workshop toolkit, Basic About/PhDSetu explanation
  * **OPERATIONS:** Attendee registration, Feedback system, Consent/privacy language, Workshop photography/recording permission, Post-event communications, Contact/support route



### P1 — Strongly Desirable

44

North Star metric

  * "Researchers taking informed action on career possibilities they had not previously considered"
  * Not clicks, not page views, not job listings, not certificates
  * Career possibility → informed action



45

Product metrics should reflect the methodology

  * DISCOVER: Career explored → TRANSLATE: Capability identified → VALIDATE: Story/JD/person investigated → PREPARE: Evidence/pitch built → ACT: Career Experiment completed → OUTCOME: Application/conversation/interview/transition
  * Much more meaningful than conventional content analytics



46

Internal rule for roadmap

  * Every new feature should answer: "Which step of the Research → Capability → Possibility → Action journey does this improve?"
  * If nobody can answer clearly: don't build it



47

Internal rule for content

  * Every piece of content should answer: "What should the researcher understand or do differently after consuming this?"
  * If answer is "They'll be inspired" — probably insufficient



48

Internal rule for workshops

  * Every ~10 minutes ask: "What is the audience doing?"
  * If answer repeatedly is "Listening to me" — redesign



49

Internal rule for community

  * Don't ask "How do we increase posts?"
  * Ask: "Are researchers exchanging knowledge that makes career decisions easier?"
  * Five excellent discussions > 500 "Hi everyone" posts



50

Internal rule for monetization

  * Ask: "Are we charging for additional value—or artificially restricting essential value?"
  * Protects the mission



51

Internal rule for AI

  * AI should help researchers: extract, organize, compare, question, explore, practice
  * Should not pretend to know: "The correct career for you is X"
  * PhDSetu model: AI proposes hypotheses → Humans investigate → Evidence informs decisions
  * Exactly aligned with research thinking
