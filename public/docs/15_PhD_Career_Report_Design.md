Section 01

## Report Objectives & Strategic Value

Why PhDSetu must publish India's first dedicated PhD career outcomes report, and what it unlocks for the platform, the community, and the national conversation on research careers.

🏅

### PR & Earned Media

A data-backed annual report on PhD career outcomes is inherently newsworthy. Indian media covers education aggressively, and no publication currently owns this data niche. Expected outcomes:

  * Headlines citing PhDSetu salary data in The Hindu, Indian Express, Times of India education supplements
  * TV panel invitations for PhDSetu founder on NDTV, India Today debates about "PhD employability crisis"
  * Wire-service pickups (PTI, ANI) distributing findings to 500+ regional outlets nationwide
  * Yearly citation cycle -- each new report re-inserts PhDSetu into the national conversation



📊

### Credibility & Authority

Positioning PhDSetu as the definitive source on Indian PhD career data transforms it from a community platform into a knowledge institution:

  * UGC and ANRF reference the report in policy circulars, lending quasi-governmental authority
  * Doctoral programs link to it as the standard career outcomes reference for incoming students
  * Industry recruiters use salary benchmarks to calibrate PhD hiring offers
  * International equivalence -- becomes India's answer to Nature PhD Survey and NSF SDR



📈

### Proprietary Data Asset

The longitudinal dataset created by annual surveys becomes PhDSetu's most valuable intellectual property:

  * Multi-year trend data on PhD salary growth, sector migration, skill evolution
  * Granular cross-tabulations unavailable anywhere else -- discipline × gender × region × institution tier
  * Monetisable through premium reports, custom analyses for recruiters, and consulting for universities
  * Research papers co-authored with academic partners using the dataset, citing PhDSetu



🤝

### Institutional Trust

The report becomes the handshake that opens doors to every stakeholder PhDSetu needs:

  * Universities share the report with their doctoral students, driving organic platform signups
  * Government bodies (DST, MHRD, ANRF) invite PhDSetu to policy consultations
  * Employers who see PhDSetu data approach the platform for talent pipeline access
  * Scholars trust a platform that invests in understanding their reality, not just selling to them



### The Strategic Calculation

India produces more PhDs than any country except the United States and China, yet has zero dedicated, annual, publicly available data on what happens to those PhDs after graduation. The closest equivalent is the occasional MHRD report or a Times Higher Education listicle. PhDSetu fills a genuine information vacuum -- and whoever fills it first owns it permanently. Nature has published its PhD survey for 25+ years. The NSF Survey of Doctorate Recipients has run since 1973. These became unchallengeable institutions. PhDSetu's report, starting in Year 1, builds the same compounding authority curve for the Indian context.

## Survey Methodology

Learning from Nature, NSF, and Vitae — while designing specifically for the Indian doctoral ecosystem's unique structure, language diversity, and institutional hierarchy.

#### Nature PhD Survey

Springer Nature • Annual since 2011

  * Global scope: 3,200+ respondents from 90+ countries
  * Online self-selection with institutional partnerships
  * Covers satisfaction, career plans, mental health, supervision quality
  * Weighted by region, not by discipline granularity
  * Strong media strategy — results published in Nature journal itself
  * Limitation: Western-centric, underrepresents Global South PhDs



#### Survey of Doctorate Recipients

NSF/NCSES • Biennial since 1973

  * Census-level: all US doctoral graduates tracked longitudinally
  * Panel survey with 70%+ response rates via institutional mandate
  * Covers employment, salary, field of work, job satisfaction
  * Gold-standard for statistical rigour: stratified sampling, imputation models
  * Deep demographic cuts: race, gender, citizenship, disability status
  * Limitation: US-only, heavily funded by federal infrastructure



#### PhDSetu Career Report

PhDSetu • Annual from 2026

  * India-specific: designed for Indian institutional types (IIT/IISc, Central, State, Private, CSIR labs)
  * Hybrid recruitment: online + university partnerships + WhatsApp communities
  * Covers salary, employment, skills, mental health, gender, regional disparities, mentorship quality
  * Discipline-granular: STEM, Social Sciences, Humanities, Commerce, Law, Management
  * Multilingual distribution: English + Hindi + regional languages for wider reach
  * Calibrated for 2,000+ responses to achieve 95% CI / ±2.2% margin of error



### Sampling Strategy

### Stratified Quota Sampling

Rather than pure random sampling (impractical without a national PhD database), use stratified quota sampling across five key dimensions to ensure representativeness:

  * **Discipline:** Engineering & Technology (25%), Sciences (25%), Social Sciences (15%), Humanities (15%), Management/Commerce (10%), Others (10%)
  * **Institution Type:** IITs/IISc/NITs (20%), Central Universities (25%), State Universities (30%), Private Universities (15%), CSIR/DRDO Labs (10%)
  * **Geography:** North (25%), South (25%), West (20%), East (20%), Northeast (10%) — reflecting PhD enrollment distribution
  * **Gender:** Target minimum 40% women respondents (reflecting actual PhD enrollment gender ratio ~42%)
  * **Career Stage:** Currently enrolled (30%), Graduated 0-2 years (25%), Graduated 3-5 years (25%), Graduated 5+ years (20%)



### Addressing Self-Selection Bias

Online surveys inherently over-represent digitally active, urban, English-fluent respondents. Mitigation strategies:

  * **Offline collection:** Partner with 50+ university placement cells to distribute paper/regional-language surveys
  * **Incentive design:** Offer PhDSetu premium membership (3 months free) to all respondents — valuable enough to motivate, cheap enough to scale
  * **WhatsApp distribution:** Leverage guide networks and alumni groups where regional-university PhDs are most active
  * **Post-stratification weighting:** Weight responses by known PhD enrollment demographics (AISHE data) to correct for sample skew
  * **Respondent profile comparison:** Compare sample demographics against AISHE and UGC data to quantify and report any remaining bias transparently

### Questionnaire Design Principles

  * **12-15 minute completion target:** Dropout rates increase 20% for every additional 5 minutes beyond 15 minutes (SurveyMonkey benchmark data)
  * **Mobile-first design:** 70%+ of Indian internet users are mobile-primary -- every question must be thumb-friendly
  * **Skip logic:** Currently enrolled scholars skip employment/salary questions; graduates skip stipend questions -- reducing irrelevant questions per respondent to ~28
  * **Progressive disclosure:** Start with easy demographics, build to sensitive topics (salary, mental health) -- after the respondent is already invested
  * **Ethical design:** "Prefer not to answer" available on all sensitive questions; no mandatory fields on mental health or gender sections
  * **Pilot testing:** Test with 50 PhD scholars across 5 discipline groups before launch; measure completion time, dropout points, and question clarity

### Why 2,000 Responses?

For a population of 2,12,000+, a sample of 2,000 achieves a 95% confidence interval with ±2.19% margin of error for national-level estimates. This is the sweet spot between statistical rigour and Year-1 feasibility:

  * **National headline statistics** (e.g., "42% of Indian PhDs earn below ₹12 LPA") are defensible at this sample size
  * **Discipline-level breakdowns** (e.g., Engineering vs. Humanities salary comparison) require ~250 responses per group -- achievable for 6-8 major disciplines with n=2,000
  * **Cross-tabulations** (e.g., women PhDs in Chemistry at state universities) will have smaller cells (n=30-80); these are reported as directional findings with caveats, not definitive statistics
  * **Nature PhD Survey comparison:** Their 2022 edition had ~3,200 global respondents -- PhDSetu's 2,000 India-only responses provide comparable or better India coverage



### Scaling the Sample (3-Year Plan)

Each year, the survey's reach grows through compounding network effects and institutional partnerships:

  * **Year 1 (2,000):** PhDSetu community + 20 university partners + social media push -- margin of error ±2.2%
  * **Year 2 (3,500):** Year 1 report generates PR; 50+ university partners; UGC endorsement -- margin ±1.7%
  * **Year 3 (5,000+):** Established survey brand; institutional distribution; potential ANRF co-branding -- margin ±1.4%
  * **Year 5 vision (10,000+):** Approaching census-like coverage of recent graduates; sub-group analyses become robust even for small disciplines

Section 05

## Data Collection Methods

A multi-channel approach combining digital reach with institutional depth, designed to overcome the fragmentation of India's doctoral ecosystem.

💻

### Online Survey Platform

Primary collection channel -- expected to yield 60% of total responses.

  * Google Forms (free, reliable, mobile-friendly) for Year 1; migrate to Typeform or custom platform in Year 2
  * Survey hosted on PhDSetu domain for brand reinforcement
  * Auto-save progress for incomplete submissions
  * Unique respondent IDs to prevent duplicates
  * Response validation: email verification + institution confirmation



🏫

### University Partnerships

Institutional distribution for reaching scholars not active on social media -- target 25% of responses.

  * Partner with 50+ placement cells and doctoral programme coordinators
  * Provide co-branded survey links for university-specific tracking
  * Offer participating universities a custom "Your University's PhD Career Data" report as incentive
  * Leverage IIT Bombay alumni network as anchor partnership for credibility
  * Target both Tier-1 and regional universities to avoid elite-institution bias



📱

### WhatsApp & Social Media

Reaching the long tail of PhDs in regional universities and labs -- target 15% of responses.

  * Share survey through PhD WhatsApp groups (most active channel for Indian scholars)
  * LinkedIn campaign with discipline-specific messaging and scholar testimonials
  * Twitter/X academic community outreach (#PhDChat, #AcademicTwitter India)
  * Instagram/YouTube shorts explaining why the survey matters -- emotional storytelling, not just statistics
  * Peer-to-peer forwarding incentive: "Complete the survey and share with 3 PhD friends"



### Data Quality Safeguards

  * **Captcha & bot detection:** Standard CAPTCHA on form submission; flag responses completed in under 3 minutes as suspicious
  * **Consistency checks:** Cross-validate career stage vs. salary responses; flag contradictions for manual review
  * **Duplicate removal:** Match on email + institution combination; retain only the most complete submission
  * **Incomplete response handling:** Include responses with ≥70% completion in analysis; report completion rates transparently
  * **Ethical approval:** Obtain IRB/ethics clearance from a partner institution (IIT Bombay recommended) to strengthen credibility, especially for mental health questions
  * **Data privacy:** All responses anonymised before analysis; individual data never shared; comply with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023



Section 06

## Analysis Framework

From raw responses to headline findings -- the statistical and analytical methods that transform 2,000+ data points into a credible, citable report.

📊

### Descriptive Statistics & Cross-Tabulations

The backbone of the report -- clear, visual breakdowns of every key variable.

  * **Frequency distributions** for all 36 questions -- bar charts, pie charts, stacked bars
  * **Cross-tabulations:** Salary × Discipline, Salary × Institution type, Salary × Gender, Satisfaction × Sector, Time-to-employment × Discipline
  * **Comparative statistics:** Mean/median salary by subgroup, gender pay gap calculation, regional disparity indices
  * **Confidence intervals** reported for all headline statistics
  * **Tools:** Python (pandas, scipy) for analysis; Datawrapper or Flourish for publication-quality charts



📈

### Regression Analysis: Salary Determinants

The report's most cited finding will be: "What determines a PhD's salary in India?" Ordinal logistic regression on salary brackets:

  * **Dependent variable:** Salary bracket (Q15)
  * **Independent variables:** Discipline, institution type, sector, gender, career stage, region, additional certifications
  * **Key questions answered:** Does institution prestige matter more than discipline? Is the gender pay gap significant after controlling for sector? Do additional certifications significantly improve outcomes?
  * **Reporting:** Present as "factors that increase your salary bracket probability" in accessible, non-technical language



🧰

### Cluster Analysis: Career Path Patterns

Identify natural groupings among respondents to create PhD "career archetypes" -- a powerful storytelling device:

  * **Variables:** Career path, satisfaction, salary, skills valued, PhD-career alignment, mental health indicators
  * **Method:** K-means clustering on standardised variables; optimal k determined by silhouette analysis
  * **Expected archetypes:** "The Academic Lifer," "The Industry Converter," "The Reluctant Postdoc," "The Entrepreneurial PhD," "The Underemployed Scholar"
  * **Output:** Each archetype gets a narrative profile in the report -- combining data with human stories



💡

### Sentiment & Gap Analysis

Beyond numbers -- understanding the emotional landscape of Indian PhD careers.

  * **Skills gap matrix:** Plot "most valued skills" (Q19) against "skills PhD didn't teach" (Q20) to identify the training gap
  * **Satisfaction drivers:** Correlation analysis between career satisfaction (Q14) and all other variables to identify what matters most
  * **Regret index:** "Would you still do a PhD?" (Q23) cross-tabulated with every demographic -- a headline-grabbing finding
  * **Mental health heat map:** Stress levels by institution type, discipline, and gender -- sensitive but essential
  * **Brain drain indicator:** Q13 (considering leaving India) analysed by discipline and institution to identify which fields lose the most talent

### University Distribution Channel

The most powerful distribution channel is not media but institutions. Email the report (with a personalised cover letter) to the Dean of Research / Doctoral Programme Coordinator at every major university in India. Offer a free, co-branded "Your University's PhD Career Data" micro-report to any institution whose scholars participated in the survey. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle: universities promote the survey to earn their own data, which increases PhDSetu's sample size, which improves the national report, which makes the university-level data more valuable. By Year 3, universities should be requesting participation rather than being recruited.

## Year-over-Year Comparison Framework

Designing for longitudinal value from Day 1 -- so each successive edition compounds the report's authority and data depth.

#### 📑 Core Metric Consistency

These 12 metrics will be tracked identically every year, enabling trend analysis and headline comparisons:

  * Median salary by discipline (Q15 × Q2)
  * Median time-to-employment (Q10)
  * Sector distribution percentages (Q9)
  * Gender pay gap ratio (Q15 × Q5)
  * "Would do PhD again" percentage (Q23)
  * Career satisfaction mean score (Q14)
  * Top 3 most valued skills (Q19)
  * Top 3 skills gaps (Q20)
  * Stress/burnout prevalence (Q28)
  * Brain drain intention rate (Q13)
  * Mentorship access rate (Q25)
  * Employer understanding score (Q36)



#### 📈 Trend Reporting Format

From Year 2 onwards, every headline finding will be presented as a trend with directional indicators:

  * Arrow indicators: ▲ (improved), ▼ (worsened), ▶ (stable) next to each metric
  * Year-over-year percentage change for all salary and employment metrics
  * 3-year rolling averages (from Year 3) to smooth out annual noise
  * Sparkline mini-charts showing metric trajectory over all available years
  * "State of the PhD" composite index — single number summarising overall career health, tracked annually



#### 🔮 Rotating Deep-Dive Topics

While core metrics stay constant, each year features a special deep-dive into one emerging theme:

  * **Year 1 (2026):** Baseline — establishing all metrics for the first time
  * **Year 2 (2027):** Deep-dive: "The AI Impact" — how AI/ML is reshaping PhD career paths
  * **Year 3 (2028):** Deep-dive: "The ANRF Effect" — has new funding changed outcomes?
  * **Year 4 (2029):** Deep-dive: "Industry PhDs" — tracking the rise of corporate R&D hiring
  * **Year 5 (2030):** Deep-dive: "Five Years In" — longitudinal follow-up with Year 1 respondents



#### 🛠 Methodology Evolution

Planned improvements that maintain backward compatibility while increasing rigour:

  * **Year 2:** Add employer survey module (50-100 hiring managers) for demand-side data
  * **Year 2:** Migrate from Google Forms to custom survey platform with advanced skip logic
  * **Year 3:** Introduce panel component — re-survey Year 1 respondents for longitudinal tracking
  * **Year 3:** Add qualitative component — 20-30 in-depth interviews with diverse PhD career journeys
  * **Year 4:** Develop PhDSetu Career Index — a composite metric cited independently of the full report
  * **Year 5:** Publish methodology paper in an education research journal for academic validation



### The Compounding Authority Principle

The true value of the PhD Career Report is not in any single edition — it is in the compounding authority that comes from consistent, longitudinal publication. Consider the precedents: Nature's PhD Survey has been cited in over 10,000 articles, policy documents, and university guidelines. The NSF Survey of Doctorate Recipients is the foundational data source for every American science policy discussion. These became unchallengeable because they started early, stayed consistent, and never missed a year. PhDSetu's report, starting in 2026, has the potential to become the equivalent institution for Indian doctoral careers. The key commitment: publish every year, on National Science Day, without fail. After 5 years of consistent publication, no competitor can replicate the trend data. After 10 years, the report _is_ the data infrastructure for Indian PhD careers.
