## Part A — The 90-Minute Workshop

Workshop Design

## Design Principles

Seven rules that govern every slide, exercise, and minute of the 90-minute session.

1

No section longer than 8–10 minutes without audience interaction

2

Energy curve: Start high → Go deep → Lighten up → Go practical → End inspiring

3

Every framework taught = one exercise practiced

4

Humor/memes placed strategically to release tension after heavy sections

5

PhDSetu introduced naturally at the end, not as a sales pitch

6

5 key takeaways designed to be shareable (multiplier effect)

7

Slides should be large-font, visual, discussion-oriented — NOT lecture slides

"This is not a placement talk."

#### OPENING POLL — 3 min

Ice-breakerBaseline

  * "How clear are you about what you want to do after PhD/M.Tech?"
  * Show of hands: Very clear / Somewhat / No idea
  * Activity type: Audience response · Purpose: Baseline + ice-breaker



0:05–0:12

#### PART 1: RESET THE LENS — 7 min

ProvocativeThoughtful

  * "Your degree is finished. Your career isn't."
  * The IIT Ropar honest expectations question
  * Expectation vs Reality meme (humor break)
  * The Translation Problem — "The gap isn't between you and the job. It's between what you CAN do and what you can COMMUNICATE."
  * Credential vs Capability comparison



**Speaker notes:** Let the big statements land. Pause after "Your career isn't." Don't rush the credential vs capability slide — it's the thesis of the entire workshop.

0:12–0:25

#### PART 2: THE INTRODUCTION PROBLEM — 13 min

DemonstrativeHands-on

  * Same person, same CV, four introductions (Version A/B/C/D)
  * The 30-second / 2-minute / 5-minute Research Pitch concept
  * **EXERCISE:** Write YOUR 30-second introduction (5 min) — Don't start with name/degree. Start with the PROBLEM. Include ONE capability. End with what you're looking for. Share with person next to you. Get feedback.



**Speaker notes:** Roleplay Version A yourself (deliberately flat). Then do Version C with energy. The contrast teaches more than any explanation. Walk around during the exercise. Pick 2–3 to highlight (with permission).

0:25–0:33

#### PART 3: COMPANY FUNDAMENTALS — 8 min

Eye-openingPractical

  * Stock market analogy (humor: Drake meme format)
  * Company Fundamentals Checklist (9 factors)
  * How company research wins interviews (before/after example)
  * **EXERCISE:** Research one company on your phone — 3 min



**Speaker notes:** The stock market joke lands well. Let them actually Google a company. The surprise is how much is publicly available. Ask: "What surprised you?"

0:33–0:47

#### PART 4: CAREER ADJACENCY — 14 min ★ CORE

InspiringAnalyticalCreative

  * Dr. Sonali's career journey: Uranium → Textile → Haircare → Skincare → Material Science
  * "The industries changed. The capabilities travelled."
  * What capabilities stayed the same? · Career Adjacency Map concept
  * Real examples: Polymer Science, Signal Processing, Mechanical Engineering
  * The 4-Question Framework
  * **EXERCISE:** Build YOUR Adjacency Map (7 min) — Center: My Research · 4 branches: Techniques / Problems / Methods / Learning Agility · For each: 2–3 industries or roles
  * Meme break after exercise



**Speaker notes:** This is the HEART of the workshop. The career journey is the proof. The 4-question framework is the tool. The exercise is where "aha" moments happen. Give them the full 7 minutes. Don't rush.

0:47–0:50

#### PART 5: INDUSTRIAL TOUCH — 3 min

Quick TipBonus

  * Before-you-submit thesis checklist
  * Before/After thesis conclusion example



**Speaker notes:** Speed through this. It's a "bonus tip" that early-PhD students will love. One slide, one example, done.

0:50–0:58

#### PART 6: INTERVIEW FRAMEWORKS — 8 min

StructuredHumorous

  * Interview Triangle (Situation → Action → Evidence)
  * Evidence Bank table · Difficult questions guide (4 common ones)
  * HR vs Technical interviews comparison
  * Meme: "Leadership in PhD" joke



**Speaker notes:** Don't go deep on every difficult question. Pick 2, demonstrate the "Don't/Do" format. The meme provides relief before the next heavy section.

0:58–1:04

#### PART 7: AI-ERA ADVANTAGE — 6 min

Wake-up CallEmpowering

  * "You already know how to use ChatGPT for CVs. So does every other candidate."
  * What AI CAN'T do vs Your Unfair Advantage
  * Smart AI prompts (the researcher's way) · Career Command Center (Excel tracker)
  * What this workshop gives that AI doesn't



**Speaker notes:** This section positions the ENTIRE workshop as beyond-AI. It's meta-awareness. The Career Command Center is something they can build tonight.

1:04–1:08

#### PART 8: TIER 2/3 & SHARING — 4 min

EmpatheticActionable

  * "Your Tag Is Not Your Talent"
  * What you CAN control (8 factors)
  * 5 things to teach a friend at any university



**Speaker notes:** For IIT students, frame it as: "What would you tell a friend at a less-known university?" This builds empathy AND reinforces the tools. The "5 things to teach" slide is a multiplier mechanism.

1:08–1:18

#### PART 9: THE REAL STRUGGLE — 10 min ♥ EMOTIONAL CORE

VulnerableSupportivePractical

  * PhD Isolation Problem (B.Tech batch vs PhD solo journey)
  * Your First Network Is Already Here (department research map)
  * Keep Your Mind Active — Study Others' Journeys
  * Build Your Inner Circle (Career Buddy System + Learn from Seniors + Help Juniors)
  * The real struggle: financial pressure, age clock, invisible rejection, responsibilities
  * Survival Strategies: Parallel Tracks + Rejection Debugging
  * Peer Support System: Normalize the struggle
  * Mental Health: Things that feel true vs what's actually true + helpline numbers



**Speaker notes:** This changes the room's energy. You're acknowledging what everyone feels but nobody says. Pause after the 4 financial/emotional cards. Let people feel seen. Then move to practical strategies. The mental health slide with helpline numbers is important — don't skip it.

1:18–1:22

#### WORST CASE & DECISIONS — 4 min

PracticalReassuring

  * Worst-Case Playbook (8 alternate earning avenues)
  * 6 Thumb Rules for the tough phase
  * Two Offers Decision Framework · Offer Decision Cheat Sheet



**Speaker notes:** Speed through the earning avenues table — let them photograph it. The thumb rules are the "emergency kit." The two-offers framework is a positive note — "This is a GOOD problem."

1:22–1:26

#### PART 10: STAY SHARP — 4 min

InformativePractical

  * PhD Tunnel Vision Problem
  * Weekly Awareness Kit (30 min/week: ET, Nature Careers, LinkedIn)
  * Group Discussion formats
  * Things Nobody Tells PhD Students (rapid-fire) · More Things Worth Knowing (9 cards)



**Speaker notes:** These are "take home" slides. Don't read every card. Highlight 3–4 that surprise the audience. Let them photograph the rest.

1:26–1:28

#### 7-DAY CAREER EXPERIMENT — 2 min

Call to Action

  * One action per day for 7 days
  * "Your career lens changes in one week"



1:28–1:32

#### PART 11: TOOLKIT & PHDSETU — 4 min

ConsolidationNatural Plug

  * Complete Toolkit summary (10 tools)
  * PhDSetu introduction (Career Map, Stories, Paths, Community, Tools)
  * QR code



**Speaker notes:** The PhDSetu reveal should feel like: "Everything we discussed today — there's a platform that continues this journey." Not a sales pitch. PhDSetu is an independent platform; Dr. Sonali is the workshop resource person.

1:32–1:35

#### 5 KEY TAKEAWAYS — 3 min

  * Your capabilities are more portable than your thesis title. Map them.
  * Research companies like you research your experiments. Do the fundamentals.
  * Don't read your CV out loud. Tell the story of what you can solve.
  * Your career won't follow your thesis topic. That's not a bug — it's a feature.
  * Help one other researcher see this. That's how we build a network that works for everyone.



"Share these with a friend who needs to hear them."

1:35–1:38

#### CLOSING — 3 min

  * "Your thesis tells people what you researched. Your professional story should show them what you can do next."
  * "Where could your research take you?"
  * Thank you. Go run your first Career Experiment.



1:38–1:50

#### Q&A — 12 min

  * Open questions
  * Or: "What's ONE thing you'll do differently this week?"
  * Resources slide available · Stay connected slide with QR/links

### ⏩ If Running Short — Skip These

  * Part 5 (Industrial Touch) — 3 min saved
  * Part 8 (Tier 2/3) — 4 min saved
  * Detailed "Things Nobody Tells" — show 3 instead of 6
  * Group Discussion formats — mention, don't present

### ✂️ If Running Long — Cut These

  * Phone exercise in Part 3 (save 3 min)
  * Adjacency Map exercise from 7 to 5 min (save 2 min)

Content Strategy

## Content Strategy Principles

1

Every piece of content should answer: "What should the researcher understand or DO differently after this?"

2

If the answer is just "they'll be inspired" — insufficient

3

Use research language naturally (career hypothesis, evidence, experiment) without overdoing it

4

Start STEM-focused, but design the capability taxonomy to be discipline-agnostic

5

Don't just add career pages — recruit domain contributors who understand the discipline

6

Quality > Quantity: 15 excellent career paths > 100 generic ones

7

Stories should show capability discovery, not just job placement

8

All content should pass the Inclusion Test (access, ability, language, institution, discipline, money, geography)

### Pricing Considerations

  * Free tier must provide enough value that users recommend PhDSetu
  * Membership should feel like "I get MORE" not "I'm paying for what should be free"
  * Suggested: ₹99–199/month or ₹999–1999/year (PhD student-friendly)
  * Institutional pricing for universities: ₹25,000–50,000/year for campus-wide access
