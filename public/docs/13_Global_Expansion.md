## The Global PhD Career Infrastructure Gap

The crisis PhDSetu solves in India exists everywhere. Doctoral researchers worldwide face the same structural isolation, career information asymmetry, and network poverty. No global platform addresses this comprehensively.

🌎

### Universal Pain Point

Across 190+ countries, PhD researchers work under individual supervisors with no cohort-based career support. The isolation model of doctoral training creates identical career gaps everywhere -- from Dhaka to Nairobi to Sao Paulo.

📈

### Market Scale

The global career guidance platform market is valued at $4.19 billion (2026), projected to reach $8.32 billion by 2035 at a CAGR of 7.9%. The PhD-specific segment -- currently underserved -- represents a $400M+ opportunity within this market.

🏠

### No Incumbent at Scale

EURAXESS covers Europe. VersatilePhD and Beyond the Professoriate are US-centric. No platform serves the Global South -- where 60% of the world's new PhDs are being produced. PhDSetu can own this space.

### The Strategic Thesis

PhDSetu starts as India's research career bridge, but the product -- career translation, peer community, mentorship matching, and skill development for doctoral researchers -- is universally needed. India's 3.5M+ diaspora researchers become the distribution channel: they are already in the universities, labs, and companies of every target country. The playbook is not "export an Indian product" -- it is "build the global infrastructure that only India can seed."

## Three-Wave Expansion Strategy

PhDSetu expands in concentric rings from India, leveraging cultural proximity, diaspora bridges, and shared academic traditions to minimize entry friction at each stage.

### Global Expansion Footprint -- 2026-2031

A phased rollout targeting 13 countries across three continents, following the path of least cultural and structural resistance.

Wave 1 -- Y1-Y2

#### South Asia

Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal. Shared academic DNA with India. English-medium universities. Strong IIT/IISc alumni presence. Minimal localisation needed beyond local university partnerships.

Wave 2 -- Y2-Y3

#### Southeast Asia

Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam. Large PhD populations. Growing R&D ecosystems. English proficiency varies -- Philippines high, Vietnam lower. University partnerships critical.

Wave 3a -- Y3-Y5

#### Sub-Saharan Africa

Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa. Massive unmet demand. English-speaking markets. ACU (Association of Commonwealth Universities) provides partnership framework. Fast-growing PhD enrollment.

Wave 3b -- Y3-Y5

#### North Africa & MENA

Egypt. Largest PhD-producing nation in Africa/MENA. Strong STEM tradition. Arabic localisation required. Government university system dominates. Indian diaspora present in Gulf as bridge.

Wave 3c -- Y4-Y5

#### Latin America

Brazil, Mexico, Argentina. Large research ecosystems. Portuguese/Spanish required. CAPES (Brazil) and CONACYT (Mexico) are key institutional partners. Strong public university tradition.

### Scoring Methodology

Each dimension is scored 1-10 based on publicly available data, researcher interviews, and institutional analysis. **PhD Population** reflects addressable market size. **Language Ease** measures compatibility with PhDSetu's current English-first platform. **Diaspora Bridge** captures the strength of the Indian researcher community in that country as an acquisition channel. **Market Gap** evaluates the absence of competing PhD-specific career infrastructure. **Growth Rate** captures the trajectory of doctoral enrollment, favouring countries investing in R&D expansion. Weights are designed to prioritise markets where PhDSetu can achieve product-market fit fastest with minimal localisation investment.

## EURAXESS Model Study

EURAXESS (European Researcher Mobility Portal) is the closest global analogue to PhDSetu's vision. Operating across 43 European countries and 9 worldwide hubs, it offers critical lessons in what to learn -- and what to do differently.

### What EURAXESS Is

Launched in 2003 by the European Commission, EURAXESS is a pan-European initiative supporting researcher mobility and career development. It operates as a network of over 600 Service Centres in 43 countries, providing personalised information on visas, social security, taxation, and employment rights. The portal lists 10,000+ research positions, 1,000+ funding opportunities, and connects researchers with over 100,000 registered users. Budget: approximately €30M annually (EC-funded). Staff: ~1,200 across all participating countries (most part-time, seconded from universities).

### What to Learn from EURAXESS

  * **Network of local nodes:** EURAXESS succeeds because it has physical Service Centres in each country, staffed by people who understand local regulations. PhDSetu needs "Country Champions" who serve this function digitally.
  * **Charter & Code:** The European Charter for Researchers and Code of Conduct for Recruitment creates a shared standard. PhDSetu should develop an equivalent "Research Career Standards Framework" for participating institutions.
  * **Dual-sided marketplace:** EURAXESS serves both researchers (career seekers) and institutions (recruiters), creating a two-sided network effect. PhDSetu must maintain this balance.
  * **Government backing:** EC funding gives EURAXESS legitimacy and sustainability. PhDSetu should seek ANRF/DST/UGC partnership for similar institutional backing.
  * **Standardised researcher profiles:** The "EURAXESS Profile" is a lightweight but structured researcher CV. This validates PhDSetu's "Research Passport" concept.



### What to Do Differently

  * **Community over portal:** EURAXESS is a portal, not a community. No peer interaction, no mentorship matching, no social features. PhDSetu's community-first approach is the key differentiator.
  * **Career translation, not just listing:** EURAXESS lists jobs but doesn't help researchers translate their skills for industry. PhDSetu's AI-powered career translation is a unique capability.
  * **Revenue sustainability:** EURAXESS is 100% publicly funded, making it vulnerable to budget cuts. PhDSetu's hybrid model (freemium + institutional subscriptions + recruiter fees) is more resilient.
  * **Mobile-first:** EURAXESS is desktop-optimised, designed for European internet infrastructure. PhDSetu must be mobile-first for South Asian, African, and Southeast Asian markets where smartphone is the primary device.
  * **Speed of execution:** EURAXESS took 10 years to reach 43 countries with government backing. PhDSetu must move faster with a lean model: digital-first, diaspora-distributed, no physical centres.
  * **Global South focus:** EURAXESS covers Europe + 9 hubs (mainly in developed countries). The Global South -- where 60% of new PhDs are produced -- is entirely unserved. PhDSetu owns this space.



### The EURAXESS Gap = PhDSetu's Opportunity

EURAXESS proves that a multi-country research career platform can work at scale. But it is government-funded, Euro-centric, portal-first, and community-last. PhDSetu inverts every one of these properties: privately sustainable, Global-South-first, mobile-first, and community-first. The market EURAXESS doesn't serve -- 3+ million doctoral researchers in Asia, Africa, and Latin America -- is precisely PhDSetu's addressable market.

## Global Expansion SWOT Analysis

A strategic assessment of PhDSetu's positioning for international expansion.

### Strengths

  * India produces the world's second-largest doctoral cohort -- home market validates product-market fit at scale before expansion
  * 3.5M+ Indian diaspora researchers are an unmatched distribution channel -- no competitor has this built-in global network
  * Community-first model creates defensible moats that portal-first competitors (EURAXESS) cannot replicate
  * Founder's personal journey (BARC -> MNC -> NGO) is universally relatable to PhD researchers everywhere
  * Low-cost India engineering team enables competitive pricing for Global South markets
  * Research Passport concept fills a gap no existing platform addresses



### Weaknesses

  * No existing brand recognition outside India -- must build credibility from zero in each new market
  * Hindi/Sanskrit name ("Setu") may not resonate immediately in non-South Asian markets
  * Limited non-English language capabilities in founding team
  * No existing relationships with universities or governments outside India and immediate neighbours
  * Cash-constrained startup cannot invest in simultaneous multi-market launches
  * PhD career infrastructure is a niche market -- investor excitement may be limited compared to broader edtech



### Opportunities

  * Complete whitespace -- no PhD-specific career platform exists for Asia, Africa, or Latin America
  * Global PhD enrollment growing at 5-7% annually, with fastest growth in target markets
  * NEP 2020 (India), LPDP (Indonesia), Project 89 (Vietnam), TETFUND (Nigeria) creating massive government-funded scholar cohorts who need career services
  * AI capabilities (career translation, mentorship matching) improve dramatically each year, making the product exponentially more valuable
  * Impact investing sector increasingly interested in "education infrastructure for the Global South" -- PhDSetu fits this thesis perfectly
  * BRICS+ academic cooperation framework provides institutional channel to South Africa, Brazil, and potentially Egypt



### Threats

  * EURAXESS expanding to Global South -- if the EC decides to extend its model to Africa/Asia, it arrives with government legitimacy and deep pockets
  * LinkedIn investing in research-specific features could capture the PhD career space as a feature within a larger platform
  * Local competitors emerging after PhDSetu validates the market in each country (copy-and-localise strategy)
  * Currency volatility in target markets (Argentina, Nigeria, Egypt) could erode revenue unpredictably
  * Regulatory tightening (Vietnam, Indonesia data laws) could increase compliance costs and slow expansion
  * Brain drain narratives could turn governments against foreign career platforms perceived as facilitating talent outflow
